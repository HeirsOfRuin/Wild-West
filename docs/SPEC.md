# Dead or Alive: one-page spec

Stage 1 record. Written from the author's concept ("A wild west outlaw: Game
Concept (one page)", October 2026). Where this page and the concept differ, the
difference is called out and the reason given.

## Setting
Western Missouri, spring 1866 to winter 1882. You make an outlaw in the mould of
the James-Younger gang: an ex-bushwhacker, a farmer's son, a cavalryman or a
drifter who cannot go home to the life he had. (The first build cast you as Jesse
James with the whole gang; the sandbox round, `docs/FRAME-SANDBOX.md`, replaced
that with a character you make and a gang you build.) It is a game because the gang lived on two things it
could not buy: the counties that hid it and the men who rode with it. Both wear
out.

## Core loop (one season)
1. **Scout** (optional, $40, up to two a season): see a target's true guard, town
   and cash this year. Unscouted, you see a range around its reputation.
2. **Plan**: who rides, what each man does (inside, street, horses), quiet or
   bold, where you run to, how the take is split.
3. **Execute**: the alarm, the vault, the street, the horses.
4. **Escape and hide**: the posse chases you to the hideout you chose.
5. **Consequences**: sympathy, heat, Pinkertons, reward, legend and every man's
   loyalty move.

A season can instead be spent lying low (and giving money away), or living as
Mr. Thomas Howard under cover. Scouting, letters to the paper, pay, recruiting
and dismissals do not end the season.

## The scarce resource
**Goodwill**, in two forms that drain on their own: the counties' sympathy (which
decides where you can hide and who informs on you) and your men's loyalty (which
decides who sells you). Money is plentiful by comparison; what money cannot fix is
a hostile county or a man who has already talked to the law. This differs from
the earlier games: Free Company's scarce resource was men and contracts, Silk
Road's was capital and time.

## Per-turn decision
Ride or hide. Riding feeds the men (broke men turn), raises legend (Southern
sympathy) and money, but heats counties, raises the reward and risks blood. Hiding
cools the posses and the Pinkertons but bleeds money and the men's patience. When
you ride, the plan is the decision: scouting changes what the right plan is.

## Ways to lose
- **Killed**, on a job or when they come for you.
- **Captured**, the same.
- **Betrayed**: a man who has turned leads a posse to your door, or (the Bob Ford
  arc) shoots you himself.

## The one way to win
Retire, once married (there has to be somebody to quit for): the gang dissolved, a believed cover as Mr. Howard, $5,000 saved, the wife and children
alive. Then survive the quiet years after: eight seasons plus one for every 10
points of peak legend. Old gang members who left broke and bitter are the danger.

## Score
Years survived, money taken and kept, legend, townsfolk killed, and the men who
died in your service, listed by name. Shown as parts, not summed.

## Scope (first playable, as the concept set it)
One state (6 counties), 6 towns, 12 targets of 3 kinds, 2-4 companions you
choose, generated recruits plus 9 historical "known men", gear and safe houses,
the three pressure tracks, sympathy across 6 counties, the
Bob Ford betrayal arc plus the general turncoat mechanic, and Northfield as a
one-time offer in 1876.

## Differences from the concept, and why
- **Rocklin, Iowa** in the concept: the James-Younger gang's first train robbery
  was at **Adair, Iowa**, 21 July 1873, on the Chicago, Rock Island & Pacific.
  "Rocklin" looks like a blend of "Rock Island". Iowa is outside the one-state
  scope, so 1873 is the year Missouri train jobs open.
- **Northfield** is outside Missouri; kept as a one-season offer from Bill
  Chadwell, because the concept names it and it teaches the scouting lesson the
  hard way.
- **Retirement needs a wife.** In the first build it opened in 1874, when Jesse
  married Zee; without a lock, the first kill gate showed a careful player
  retiring after one job in 1866. In the sandbox, marrying is your choice ($300).

## What carries over / what does not
The engine shape (seasons, a job resolver, pressure tracks, sympathy map, hidden
loyalty) is meant to carry to the later Depression-era mode (Dillinger, 1933-34):
cars for horses, radio and telephone for the telegraph, state lines as escape
routes, and an FBI that modernises. Nothing from that mode is built yet.
